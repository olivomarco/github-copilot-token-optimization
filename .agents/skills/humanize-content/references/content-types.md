# Content Types

Read the section matching the confirmed content type for drafting or revision. These are genre-specific choices; the skill's reader-need test and preservation rules remain authoritative.

## Technical/docs

Favor precise explanations of behavior, prerequisites, and limitations. Keep domain terms that distinguish real concepts. Expand compressed wording when readers would otherwise have to infer an actor, condition, or sequence. Separate an instruction from its rationale when that improves scanning. Preserve the order of operational steps.

For a current-use guide, keep the supported procedure and remove obsolete alternatives. For example, if the source establishes the current storage setup and marks an older `fstab` example as superseded, delete the old example and its reconciliation commentary. A paragraph saying "The original text is retained as a record, not a validated configuration to apply" keeps the cleanup burden on the reader. If an unverified mapping is still needed for the active procedure, surface that specific blocker; ask for the correct mapping rather than inventing one or hiding the risk. Keep historical detail when the document's purpose is migration or history.

Professional technical writing can use ordinary verbs and explanatory phrasing. Keep clear instructions such as "Schedule daily short tests at 02:00 on your first three HDDs, preserving your original configuration." Do not replace them with denser wording such as "Retain your original daily short-test schedule of 02:00 for the first three HDDs" merely to sound more formal. Likewise, "Distribute HDD long tests across the week, rather than running them simultaneously" does not need a synonym swap to "Stagger." The original and the alternative may both be valid; without a clear reader benefit, retain the original.

**Source**

> This powerful cache seamlessly accelerates lookups. Entries may remain stale for up to 30 seconds.

**Professional**

> The cache speeds up lookups. Entries may remain stale for up to 30 seconds.

The rewrite removes promotional framing while retaining the freshness limit. Keep claims qualitative when the source supplies no benchmark.

## Email/message

Make the purpose and requested action easy to find. Match the relationship's level of politeness. Keep recipients, deadlines, requests, and promises distinct: a request is not an obligation, and a possibility is not a commitment. A genuine offer such as "If you need more time, let me know" gives the recipient an option; keep it when that option matters.

**Source**

> I wanted to reach out to ask whether you might be able to review the draft by Thursday. If not, Friday also works.

**Direct**

> Could you review the draft by Thursday? Friday also works.

Both acceptable dates survive. The shorter version still asks rather than demands.

## Article/blog

Make the line of argument easy to follow. Remove introductions and conclusions that merely repeat the thesis. Retain qualifications, attributed views, and contrasts that the argument depends on, including transitions that make those relationships clear. A stronger opening can foreground an existing point without inventing a hook.

**Source**

> It is important to note that, in our six interviews, four participants preferred the shorter form. This may suggest that a shorter form is worth testing.

**Conversational**

> In our six interviews, four participants preferred the shorter form. A shorter form may be worth testing.

The small sample and tentative conclusion remain visible.

## Social post

Bring forward the existing point and remove expendable setup. Keep platform limits supplied by the user. Use line breaks, hashtags, emoji, and calls to action only when they fit the source or an explicit request; a social post need not become an engagement pitch.

**Source**

> I am excited to share that I have finally shipped my first plugin after three weekends of work. I am not sure yet whether anyone else will find it useful.

**Personal/editorial**

> I finally shipped my first plugin after three weekends of work. I'm excited, though I'm not sure yet whether anyone else will find it useful.

The voice draws from the author's stated experience and emotion; it adds no users, outcomes, or endorsements.
